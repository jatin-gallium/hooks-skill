# Brief — Acknowledgment / Gratitude Post

Use for shout-outs to a group, community thanks, "people who…" posts.

## Pre-fills

- **Best formats**: Dense (with a specific, relatable first item cut off), Punchy+Context.
- **Angle bias**: Rewards the reader for showing up. Make them feel seen, not taught.
- **Rules to emphasize**: Rule 33 (acknowledgment list — the list itself is the hook).
- **Register**: Match author voice; lowercase works here when authentic.

## Brief

```
BRIEF: acknowledgment

## Raw material

- The opening line ("I just wanted to acknowledge people who:" or similar):
  <<...>>

- The first (specific, cut-off-ready) item that earns the "...see more" click:
  <<...>>

- 3–5 more items to include in the body (don't reveal in the hook):
  <<...>>

- Why these people deserve the acknowledgment (ONE sentence max):
  <<...>>

## Audience

- Primary audience: <<B2B | personal-brand | mixed>>
- Niche: <<#tag>>
- The in-group being acknowledged: <<...>>

## Author voice

- Register: <<standard | lowercase>>
- Voice notes:
  <<...>>

## Format bias

- Prefer: <<Dense | Punchy+Context>>
- Media attached: <<none>>

## Rules to emphasize

Rule 33 (acknowledgment list rewards the reader for showing up).

## Constraints

- The first item must be specific enough that a reader thinks "wait, is that me?"
- Do NOT open with "shout-out to…" — overused.
- No em dashes.

## Output request

Generate 8–10 hooks. All should cut off mid-first-item. Recommend top 3.
```

## How to use this brief well

- **The power is in the first item being cut off.** The reader clicks to see if they're included.
- **No humblebragging.** If the acknowledgment is really about your audience reach, it reads fake.
- **Specificity beats warmth.** "To everyone who…" is weak. "To the people who reply to every DM but still feel invisible" is strong.
- **Examples to study**:
  - Matt Barker "Shout out to everyone posting on LinkedIn with less than 500 followers and no engagement."
