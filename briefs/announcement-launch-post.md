# Brief — Announcement / Launch Post

Use for new product, feature, event, cohort, book, podcast, conference.

## Pre-fills

- **Best formats**: Punchy+Context (origin-story angle), Dense (if there's a specific stat), Single-Line Bomb (anti-hype admission).
- **Angle bias**: Origin story, problem it solves, anti-hype admission. NEVER open with "Excited to announce" or the brand/product name.
- **Rules to emphasize**: Rule 21 (anti-hype), Rule 30 (meta-hook), Rule 32 (problem-origin), Rule 33 (acknowledgment pre-frame).
- **Register**: Match brand voice. Personal brand launches often lowercase; B2B usually standard.

## Brief

```
BRIEF: announcement-launch

## Raw material

- The shared frustration this thing solves:
  <<...>>

- The origin moment — when/where the idea actually started (a note in the phone, a 9pm Slack, a conference):
  <<...>>

- Anti-hype admission I'm willing to make (low stakes, uncertainty, nobody cares yet):
  <<...>>

- A specific number attached to the thing (price, cohort size, dates, first N users):
  <<...>>

- Who it is NOT for (exclude openly to intensify for who it IS for):
  <<...>>

## Audience

- Primary audience: <<B2B | personal-brand | mixed>>
- Niche: <<#tag>>
- Why the reader should care in one sentence (from THEIR view):
  <<...>>

## Author voice

- Register: <<standard | lowercase>>
- Brand voice notes:
  <<...>>

## Format bias

- Prefer: <<Punchy+Context | Single-Line Bomb | Dense>>
- Avoid: Stacked — announcements rarely fit the pattern.
- Media attached: <<none | image | video | carousel>>

## Rules to emphasize

Rule 21 (anti-hype admission), Rule 30 (meta-hook self-aware tease),
Rule 32 (problem-origin), Rule 24 (audience segmentation — "this is for X, normies keep scrolling").

## Constraints

- HARD BAN: "I'm excited to announce", "I'm humbled to share", "thrilled to", "big news".
- Do NOT open with the product/brand name.
- No em dashes.

## Output request

Generate 8–10 hooks. At least 2 use Rule 21 (anti-hype). At least 1 uses Rule 32 (problem-origin). Recommend top 3.
```

## How to use this brief well

- **The origin story is the hook.** "A year ago this was a note in my phone" beats every "excited to announce" ever written.
- **Admit the small stakes.** "I'm putting it here, so it's likely not a single person will see this" went viral because it was the opposite of launch-speak. See Rule 21.
- **Don't name the product in the hook.** Let the reader click first.
- **If you name who it's NOT for, name them specifically.** "(normies, keep scrolling)" works because it's mean in a playful way.
