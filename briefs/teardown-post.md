# Brief — Teardown / Analysis Post

Use when you're publicly analyzing an ad, post, landing page, launch, company, or public artifact.

## Pre-fills

- **Best formats**: Dense, Punchy+Context. Stacked sub-variant E (problem-cost-twist) works if the teardown has a clean three-beat arc.
- **Angle bias**: Name the artifact, name the result, hint at the non-obvious lesson.
- **Rules to emphasize**: Rule 39 (scene-setting without explaining the joke), Rule 20 (contrarian reframe of why it worked/failed), Rule 26 (authority + constraint in the setup).
- **Register**: Standard.

## Brief

```
BRIEF: teardown

## Raw material

- The artifact being torn down (post / ad / landing page / launch / company / campaign):
  <<...>>

- The public result (viral, flopped, revenue, views):
  <<...>>

- The counterintuitive lesson (what most observers would miss):
  <<...>>

- The one line from the artifact that's most quotable:
  <<...>>

- What the reader should check in their OWN work after reading:
  <<...>>

## Audience

- Primary audience: <<B2B | marketer | founder | creator>>
- Niche: <<#tag>>
- The reader's question: <<"What does this mean for my own [thing]?">>

## Author voice

- Register: <<standard>>
- Voice notes (dry analyst, hot-take energy, etc.):
  <<...>>

## Format bias

- Prefer: <<Dense | Punchy+Context | Stacked-E>>
- Media attached: <<none | screenshot | carousel>>

## Rules to emphasize

Rule 39 (scene-setting), Rule 20 (contrarian reframe),
Rule 26 (authority + filtering).

## Constraints

- No em dashes.
- Do NOT explain the lesson in the hook — open the curtain, don't explain the play (Rule 39).
- Do NOT sneer at the artifact's creator; analyze, don't dunk.

## Output request

Generate 8–10 hooks. At least 1 scene-setting open (Rule 39).
At least 1 Dense with the result up front. Recommend top 3.
```

## How to use this brief well

- **Name the artifact precisely in the body, not the hook.** The hook hints; the expansion reveals.
- **Teardown posts win on the non-obvious takeaway.** If the lesson is "good copy works," skip the post.
- **The "scene-setting two-liner" is gold for teardowns** — sets up tension without giving it away.
