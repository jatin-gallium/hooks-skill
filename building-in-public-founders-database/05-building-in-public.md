### #064 — Y Combinator Fall 2025 — what "making it" actually cost

**Tags:** #yc #sacrifice #origin-story #from-zero #grandparents
**Type:** Story
**Date:** Sep 25, 2025
**Engagement:** 296 reactions, 111 comments, 5 reposts (Score: 2,600)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7372325011853905322

Y Combinator Fall 2025. We made it.

But let me tell you what "making it" actually cost.

In the last 6 months building Imagine AI:
→ I dropped out of my Master's program
→ Neo Lee left his founding engineer position
→ We moved apartments 3 times (startup life = constantly broke)
→ I went through the toughest breakup of my life
→ Our server crashed for 2 days and nobody noticed

We sacrificed everything. Sleep. Stability. Relationships. Sanity.

Why? Because we're solving a $10B problem.

We built Imagine AI (YC F25) - an agentic AI that becomes your company's Head of Content.

We perform deep research, build a 100-page persona of you, and create content that actually books meetings at named accounts.

One client just closed a $5M deal from a single post.

From 0 to 14K revenue in 8 weeks.

Now we're part of YC F25.

Sometimes I think about the distance I've traveled to get here. My grandparents' bathroom in rural China doesn't have running water. You heat water on a stove to shower. That's where my family started.

The gap between that reality and standing here today shouldn't be possible. But when you've seen what "starting from zero" really looks like, you realize that sacrifice isn't a cost -> it's an investment.

To every founder choosing the fire over comfort: the climb is worth it.

### #065 — We spent $10,070 moving offices — leverage decisions

**Tags:** #office #leverage #time-management #growth
**Type:** Story + Lesson
**Date:** Feb 22, 2026
**Engagement:** 294 reactions, 69 comments (Score: 2,160)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7431729811431129089

We spent $10,070 moving offices last week.

$620 on the move itself. $9,450 on equipment we never had before.

Tables. Chairs. Plates. Cups. Utensils. Trash cans. A whiteboard. Actual light. Monitors.

Before this, we were running Imagine AI out of 946 Stockton Street in Chinatown. It was our first SF office and where we lived. Most magnificent view in the city.

But the team is outgrowing the space. You can't contain us in a one-bedroom anymore.

Here's the thing about moving as a startup: every hour you spend packing boxes is an hour you're not serving customers.

So we made a decision. Instead of spending 20 hours moving ourselves, we hired movers and focused on closing deals. One deal covers the entire moving cost.

That's how we think about everything. What's the highest leverage use of your time right now?

New office. New whiteboard. Same energy. Bigger team.

### #069 — Organized first YC group office hour outside the batch

**Tags:** #accountability #yc #peer-pressure #goals
**Type:** Story + Lesson
**Date:** Mar 24, 2026
**Engagement:** 167 reactions, 31 comments (Score: 1,145)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7433194398626230272

We organized our first YC group office hour outside the batch.

Here's how it works: every two weeks during the batch, you sit in a room with other founders. You set very ambitious goals. Then you come back and report whether you hit them.

No fluff. No sugarcoating. Just: did you hit your number or not?

Most people fail their goals. That's the point. It's better to fail at something impossible than succeed at something that doesn't matter.

That peer pressure is the most underrated part of YC. The accountability of knowing someone will ask you in 14 days "so did you actually do it?" changes everything.

The batch ended. But the tradition doesn't.

I organized this one because the momentum we built during those 12 weeks is too valuable to let fade.

Who holds you accountable? If the answer is nobody, that's your biggest bottleneck.

### #074 — Founders Inc rejected us → 2 months later accepted us

**Tags:** #rejection #pivot #feedback #cal-build

**Date:** Sep 25, 2025
**Engagement:** 22 reactions, 16 comments (Score: 270)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7366137386818293760

First, Foundational Capital's Cal Build program rejected us. Then 2 months later they accepted us.

The initial feedback was a gut punch: Wrong idea. Little traction. Zero sticky users.

They were 100% right.

That rejection could've been the end. For us, it was the starting line.

We killed the old idea and went back to the one thing that matters: talking to our users.

This week, we got an email. We're in the next Cal Build cohort. One of only 8 teams.

What changed? We stopped building what we thought people wanted and started building what they told us they needed.

Now the traction is real:
Sticky users who are in the product daily.
Insane inbound from our own content.
A product that's being pulled out of us.

The lesson here is simple:
Brutal feedback is a gift.
Don't run from it.
Build from it.
