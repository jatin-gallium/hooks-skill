Y Combinator Fall 2025. We made it.

→ Neo Lee left his founding engineer position
→ We moved apartments 3 times (startup life = constantly broke)
→ I went through the toughest breakup of my life

The gap between that reality and standing here today shouldn't be possible. But when you've seen what "starting from zero" really looks like, you realize that sacrifice isn't a cost -> it's an investment.

To every founder choosing the fire over comfort: the climb is worth it.

**Date:** Feb 22, 2026
**Engagement:** 294 reactions, 69 comments (Score: 2,160)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7431729811431129089

We spent $10,070 moving offices last week.

$620 on the move itself. $9,450 on equipment we never had before.

Tables. Chairs. Plates. Cups. Utensils. Trash cans. A whiteboard. Actual light. Monitors.

Before this, we were running Imagine AI out of 946 Stockton Street in Chinatown. It was our first SF office and where we lived. Most magnificent view in the city.

So we made a decision. Instead of spending 20 hours moving ourselves, we hired movers and focused on closing deals. One deal covers the entire moving cost.

That's how we think about everything. What's the highest leverage use of your time right now?

New office. New whiteboard. Same energy. Bigger team.

### #069 — Organized first YC group office hour outside the batch

**Tags:** #accountability #yc #peer-pressure #goals
**Type:** Story + Lesson
**Date:** Mar 24, 2026
**Engagement:** 167 reactions, 31 comments (Score: 1,145)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7433194398626230272

We organized our first YC group office hour outside the batch.

Here's how it works: every two weeks during the batch, you sit in a room with other founders. You set very ambitious goals. Then you come back and report whether you hit them.

No fluff. No sugarcoating. Just: did you hit your number or not?

Most people fail their goals. That's the point. It's better to fail at something impossible than succeed at something that doesn't matter.

That peer pressure is the most underrated part of YC. The accountability of knowing someone will ask you in 14 days "so did you actually do it?" changes everything.

The batch ended. But the tradition doesn't.

I organized this one because the momentum we built during those 12 weeks is too valuable to let fade.

Who holds you accountable? If the answer is nobody, that's your biggest bottleneck.

### #137 — We used to cook every meal — now meal service to save time

**Tags:** #frugality #time-vs-money #evolution #food

**Date:** Jun 27, 2025
**Engagement:** 155 reactions, 33 comments, 5 reposts (Score: 1,115)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7332896851588775938

We used to cook every meal to save money. Now we use a meal service to save time.

As a scrappy team, Andy and I handled everything ourselves. Every two weeks, we'd go to Costco — spending over 3 hours planning what to buy. On top of that, 1-2 hours every day cooking and doing dishes.

We saved money (around $900/month for three of us), ate healthy, avoided takeout. But the hidden cost was time: over 25 hours a week on food-related tasks.

For early-stage founders: frugality is important, but time is your most valuable resource.
