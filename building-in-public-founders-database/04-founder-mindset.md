### #042 — Two years ago I was Student Body President — felt like a failure

**Tags:** #founder-journey #resilience #origin-story #identity
**Type:** Story
**Date:** Jun 27, 2025
**Engagement:** 398 reactions, 66 comments, 9 reposts (Score: 2,668)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7334277425368834048

Two years ago, I wrapped up my term as Student Body President at UCSD - after being featured in the Wall Street Journal.

And still, I felt like a failure.

We moved mountains:
- Helped secure a $150M investment from the UC Regents for Ridge Walk North
- Led efforts that resulted in the Chancellor personally donating $4,000 toward student scholarships
- Championed the university's 2030 goal for a four-year housing guarantee

Then came the freefall.

I had twelve tabs open in my head: UCSA President. UCDC. Corporate in SF. Svshi (my startup). Nonprofit. Law firm. Senatorial campaign. Congressional campaign. Capitol Fellows. Break the Outbreak. Some plans were half-built. Some pure chaos. All felt urgent.

I applied to 50+ jobs. Got ghosted from most. CTOs quit. Vision blurred. Bank account flatlined.

Now I'm in my Master of International Affairs. Still studying for law school, but realized I wasn't focused enough the first time.

And my friend Neo said, "Why not build again?"

So we did. We started Imagine AI.

It made my hands shake again. Made my heart race. Like life had texture again.

We're still early. Still scrappy. But this time: it's not about proving anything. It's about building from truth.

If you're in the fall right now: let yourself fall. But remember: at some point, you've got to choose a direction. Focus isn't just about ambition. It's about survival. It's how you climb.

### #051 — "The Power Law" — liberation capital and embracing risk

**Tags:** #books #venture-capital #risk #power-law
**Type:** Book Review
**Date:** Mar 24, 2026
**Engagement:** 71 reactions, 10 comments (Score: 455)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7432832004754407424

I read "The Power Law" by Sebastian Mallaby on my flight to Korea.

In 1957, eight engineers defected from Shockley Semiconductor. An investor named Arthur Rock didn't just fund them. He counseled them to quit together and start Fairchild Semiconductor.

Rock called it "liberation capital."

Not just money. A belief that talent trapped under bad leadership is the biggest waste in technology.

Mallaby's core insight: venture capital follows a power law distribution. A small number of bets generate returns that dwarf everything else combined. The best way to manage risk is to embrace it fearlessly.

Every founder reading this is making the same bet. You left something stable to build something uncertain because you believed the upside was worth the risk.

That's the power law in action.

### #055 — 4 phases every startup must nail before PMF

**Type:** Framework
**Date:** Sep 25, 2025
**Engagement:** 64 reactions, 4 comments (Score: 360)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7373808656041234432

My co-founder Neo Lee just dropped a truth bomb on me: "You can't skip phases, Sky."

Four phases every startup has to nail before hitting product-market fit:

Phase I: Speed Mode — Pick up your tools, set aggressive goals, move fast.

Phase II: Detective Mode — Stop building. Start learning. Who's your actual customer? What's their real problem?

Phase III: Craftsman Mode — You do unscalable things. You iterate until 50-60% of a small group genuinely loves what you've built. Not likes. Loves.

Phase IV: Distribution Mode — Only NOW do you figure out scale.

The temptation to skip ahead is real. You want the TechCrunch headline. The hockey stick growth. But trying to scale a product with 10% retention is like flooring a car with no wheels.

### #063 — First principles thinking (Bezos) — stop reasoning by analogy

**Tags:** #first-principles #bezos #scaling #framework
**Type:** Framework
**Date:** Jul 27, 2025
**Engagement:** 20 reactions, 5 comments (Score: 150)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7353076307519287298

Jeff Bezos scaled Amazon into a 2.42 trillion dollar empire by obsessing over one thing: First Principles Thinking.

Most of us reason by analogy. We see a successful company and think: "They have 10 people doing X. To scale, I need 10 people doing X."

This is a trap. It's copying the 'what' without understanding the 'why'.

First-principle thinking flips the script. You break the problem down to its fundamental truths.

Truth 1: The value isn't just the output; it's capturing unique insights and strategic understanding.

Truth 2: The biggest bottleneck isn't execution; it's the disproportionate effort in foundational discovery and problem definition.

Truth 3: Deep, unbiased understanding of the core challenge is the most valuable, untapped asset.

The question changes from "How do I hire more people?" to "How do I build a system to mine for fundamental insights and optimize core processes at scale?"

Stop reasoning by analogy. Start reasoning from first principles.

### #118 — Early-stage growth depends on hours you put in

**Tags:** #work-ethic #routine #growth #burnout

**Date:** Apr 23, 2025
**Engagement:** 158 reactions, 18 comments, 2 reposts (Score: 974)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7308191081035141121

Early-stage startup growth depends on the hours you put in.

During YC, we worked intensely, often from 8 AM to 2 AM. The results were clear: rapid growth, improved retention, more demos, and increasing revenue.

After YC, we slowed down for a week, working only a few hours a day. Growth immediately stalled, bugs piled up, and user experience suffered.

Startup life is not a sprint. It's a race of stamina.

In the beginning, everything feels electric. Every new user felt like a win. Every tiny feature felt urgent.

But over time, the initial excitement faded — not because we cared less, but because the dopamine hits stopped landing the same way.

That's when I started to understand what startup life actually is. It's not a movie where one failure leads to a turning point. Most of the time, there's no dramatic twist. Just long stretches of building, fixing, waiting, and pushing forward.

We didn't get here from chasing highs. We got here by not giving up in the lows.
