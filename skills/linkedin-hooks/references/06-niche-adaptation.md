# 06 — Niche Adaptation

STATUS: draft

The whole point of this skill being niche-agnostic: swipes live once, but get remixed per niche on demand.

## The adaptation model

Every swipe can be split into three layers:

1. **Pattern** — the hook family (unchanged across niches).
2. **Framework** — the post shape (unchanged).
3. **Surface** — the *specific nouns, verbs, numbers, stakes, and jargon*. This layer changes per niche.

Niche adaptation = **keep pattern + framework, swap the surface**.

## The surface swap — checklist

When adapting a swipe to a new niche, systematically swap:

- [ ] **The actor** (who the story is about) — founder / sales rep / recruiter / marketer / engineer / operator / consultant.
- [ ] **The stakes** (what's on the line) — revenue / deal / hire / launch / promotion / reputation.
- [ ] **The metrics** (the numbers) — units appropriate to the niche ($ARR, close rate, cycle time, NPS, CTR, retention, time-to-ship).
- [ ] **The jargon** (terms of art) — each niche has its own. Use enough to signal belonging, not so much you shut out adjacent readers.
- [ ] **The artifacts** (what objects appear) — for SaaS it's dashboards/docs/PRs; for sales it's calls/decks/CRMs; for fitness it's reps/macros/plans.
- [ ] **The villains** (who/what the reader fights) — churn / stalled deals / bad hires / shipping bugs / plateaus.
- [ ] **The authorities** (who's referenced) — the niche's big names or roles.

## Niche voice register

Niches have different tolerance for:

- **Bluntness** — `#founder` and `#sales` tolerate more; `#hr` and `#dei` less.
- **Numbers** — `#b2b`, `#saas`, `#finance` want precise numbers; `#career`, `#coaching` tolerate more abstraction.
- **Vulnerability** — `#founder`, `#creator`, `#career` reward it; `#enterprise-sales` less.
- **Irreverence** — `#marketing`, `#creator`, `#consumer` tolerate; `#finance`, `#legal`, `#enterprise` don't.

Adjust the register of the remix accordingly, even if the pattern/framework are identical.

## Niche starter pack (placeholders to remember to swap)

When you see these placeholders in a template, they're niche-swap points:

- `[ACTOR]` — the person doing the thing.
- `[STAKES]` — what they stand to win/lose.
- `[METRIC]` — the quantified outcome.
- `[VILLAIN]` — the force opposing them.
- `[ARTIFACT]` — the object/tool/deliverable.
- `[TIMEFRAME]` — realistic for the niche.
- `[AUDIENCE]` — who the post is speaking to.
- `[JARGON]` — native terms of art.

## Adaptation workflow

When the user says "take this swipe and write it for [niche]":

1. Classify the swipe — pattern(s) + framework.
2. Extract the "bones": hook mechanism + body beats + CTA type.
3. Swap all surface elements using the checklist above.
4. Calibrate the voice register to the niche.
5. Re-check hook length against the "see more" cutoff.
6. Output: 2–3 variants at different voice registers / lengths.
