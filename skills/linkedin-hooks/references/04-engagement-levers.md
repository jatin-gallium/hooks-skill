# 04 — Engagement Levers

STATUS: draft

What the reader does after they read — the levers that turn a read into reach.

## The four engagements (ranked roughly by algorithm value)

1. **Substantive comments** — long, specific, thread-starting.
2. **Reshares with commentary** — the user is putting their name on it.
3. **Saves** — quiet but strong; signals durable value.
4. **Likes / reactions** — shallow, but they add up.

And two more that matter indirectly:

- **Profile clicks + follows** — the post is doing your positioning work.
- **DMs** — often the real conversion from a great post.

## How to earn each one

### Substantive comments

- **Ask for a specific opinion**, not a vague one. ("Which of these 5 do you disagree with?" > "Thoughts?")
- **Leave one thing unsaid** that experienced readers will want to add.
- **Present a list where one item is mildly contrarian** — invites debate.
- **Address a clearly-defined persona** — they feel seen, they comment.

### Reshares

- **Give them a quotable line.** One-line-that-fits-a-share.
- **Package a framework with a name.** People reshare named things.
- **End with a line that becomes the sharer's own caption** ("If you're a [role], read this.").

### Saves

- **Make it a reference.** Checklists, mistake lists, step-by-step frameworks.
- **Visually structured** — save-ability correlates with scannability.
- **Opens with a "save this if…"** only if the content earns it.

### Likes

- **Relatability.** "You too?" moments.
- **Identity affirmation.** "People like me do things like this."
- **Clean writing.** Readers like what's easy to read.

## Comment strategy (the creator's side)

- **Reply to every comment in the first 60–90 min.** Replies are signals.
- **Reply with depth**, not "thanks!". Ask a follow-up.
- **Pin a top comment** that adds value or links a resource.
- **Tag relevant people sparingly** and only when it genuinely adds.

## The "engagement bait" trap

Shallow bait works short-term and destroys reach long-term. Avoid:

- "Agree?" / "Thoughts?" at the end of every post.
- "Comment YES if…" formats.
- Asking for an unrelated action ("tag someone who needs this") when the post doesn't warrant it.

The rule: **engagement bait is bait the reader notices**. If they notice, you lose.

## The "one question" rule

If you're going to ask a question in the post, ask **one**. Asking three gives readers choice paralysis and none get answered.
