# 05 — Voice & Formatting

STATUS: draft

Voice + formatting are the difference between "technically good" and "actually gets read."

## Voice principles

1. **Write like you talk.** One clear voice beats polished corporate every time.
2. **Short words beat big words.** "Use" > "utilize". "Help" > "facilitate".
3. **Concrete beats abstract.** Specific nouns, verbs, numbers, names.
4. **One sentence per line.** This is a LinkedIn convention; honor it.
5. **Remove every word you can.** Tight writing = high dwell.
6. **Warmth over cleverness.** If you have to choose, choose warmth.
7. **Avoid LinkedIn-voice.** "In today's fast-paced world…", "I'm humbled to announce…" — these read as filler.

## Formatting conventions that work

### Line breaks

- Default: **one sentence per line**, with a blank line every 1–2 lines.
- Break between "beats" (idea shifts) with a full blank line.
- Don't break mid-thought; it disorients.

### Length

- First line: **< 90 characters** ideally. Definitely < 140.
- Most lines: **< 70 characters**.
- A deliberately long line can be used as a "beat" but use it sparingly.

### Lists inside posts

- Use `—`, `•`, `→`, `-`, or just hyphens. Pick one and stay consistent in the post.
- Keep each item to 1–2 lines.
- Odd number of items beats even (3, 5, 7).

### Emojis

- 0–2 per post is a safe default.
- Use them as **visual anchors** (✔, ✖, →, ⚡), not decoration.
- Avoid a full row of emojis at the top. That pattern got trained out by the feed.

### Capitalization / punctuation

- Sentence case feels human. ALL CAPS feels shouty; use for at most one word.
- Em dashes (—) are great for cadence. One per post maximum; more feels affected.
- Question marks inside hooks work sparingly.

### Numbers

- Digits > words. "7 things" > "seven things".
- Specific > round. "$47,312" > "$47k" > "about $50k".
- Anchor time precisely when you can. "Tuesday at 2:14pm" > "the other day".

## Whitespace as a design tool

LinkedIn posts are read **between scrolls**. The eye lands on the shape of the text before it reads the words. Whitespace makes the post look:

- **Scannable** → more likely to be expanded.
- **Short** → easier to start.
- **Structured** → easier to finish.

Lean on whitespace even when it feels excessive by writing-class standards.

## The "read it on mobile before posting" rule

Always preview on mobile. Mobile is where most engagement happens, and mobile wrapping will reveal:

- Hook bleeding past the "see more" line.
- Long lines that form a visual wall.
- Emoji/ASCII alignment that breaks.

## Voice calibration

Every creator has a dial between:

- **Observational** — reporting what you see (low friction, lower signature).
- **Opinionated** — arguing a point (higher friction, stronger signature).
- **Confessional** — sharing personal experience (strongest signature, highest variance).

Move along the dial deliberately by post, not by accident.
